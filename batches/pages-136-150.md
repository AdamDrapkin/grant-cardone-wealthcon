### IRC Section 7702

*Under current tax laws and for much of the past century, life insurance has offered attractive tax favored status.*

| Code Section | Description |
|---|---|
| 7702 | Defines the use of life insurance for tax-deferred growth |
| 7702A | Sets the maximum limit that can be put into a life insurance policy and receive most favorable tax treatment |
| 72 | Allows for tax-free policy withdrawals to basis from a non-MEC (modified endowment contract) policy |
| 1019(g) | Defines how a life insurance death benefit may be accelerated tax-free in the event of chronic or terminal illness |
| 101a | Allows life insurance death benefit to be passed along income tax-free to the beneficiary |

- IRC Section 7702 sets forth tests to define whether a life insurance contract may be treated as a life insurance contract for federal tax purposes.
- Implemented in 1984, the tests were enacted to limit the investment orientation of a life insurance policy.
- A life insurance contract can satisfy Section 7702 either by the Guideline Premium Test (GPT) or the Cash Value Accumulation Test (CVAT).
- Any policy that does not meet one of the two actuarial tests under Section 7702 (GPT/CVAT) will lose the favorable tax treatment of a life insurance policy.
- In 1988, Section 7702A further clarified how cash value distributions beyond basis are to be taxed if the policy is a Modified Endowment Contract (MEC).
- 7702 rates change January 2021, creating an increase in what is allowed per year into a policy, therefore potentially improving the investment side.

### Insurance and Tax Law

> 1963 - The tax court held that the cash values of life insurance, including growth, are not "constructively received" by the owner. As a result, they are not subject federal, state and local income taxes. (Theodore H. Cohen, 39 TC 1055 (1963), acq. 1964-1 CB-4)

Policy loans do not generate immediate income tax liability for the policyowner because they are not treated as distributions for tax purposes. The loan proceeds are not included in taxable income as long as the policy remains in force. However, if the policy lapses or is surrendered, the outstanding loan proceeds will be includable in gross income to the extent that the proceeds exceed the initial investment.

> 1990 GAO Report to the Chairman (Congress) clearly stated, "if a policyholder borrows the inside buildup from his or her life insurance policy, the amount borrowed is considered a transfer of capital, not a realization of income, and, therefore, is not subject to taxation. This reasoning is in accord with tax policy."
